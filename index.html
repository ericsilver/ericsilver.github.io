Eric Silver

Independent researcher — decision-making in healthcare, innovation and technology diffusion, and Medicaid.

The name aporal is for choosing among things that don't compare — apples, oranges, alligators — and for aporia, the impasse where the real question begins.

Research interests

Measures and simulations for questions that resist tidy markets and prices.

  • ΔKL — a text-based measure of innovation, drawn from trademark language.
  • O*NET-grounded models of work, tasks, and automation.
  • Simulation of non-traditional markets, with a focus on the quality of the decisions they produce.
  • Simulating the social impacts of technological change.
  • Non-monetary valuation.

Essays

Writing on decision-making, meaning, and collective rationality, from the aporal notebook.

  • Noble Medicine (2026) The moral argument for marketing of medicines.
  • Take Your Medicine (2026) “Natural” is the worst reason to set a pill down. The instinct behind the word is a contamination detector, and it is pointed at the wrong bottle.
  • "Nothing" is Expensive (2026) Goods got cheap and the moral economy of childhood inverted; we now pay for emptiness, for not-wanting, and for symbols with the referent removed.
