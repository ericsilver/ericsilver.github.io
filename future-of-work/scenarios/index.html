    What you are looking at

    Each scenario removes a share of the time people spend on work tasks and hands that work to AI or robots. Output per worker in an occupation rises by the time saved. Wages, prices and people's occupations then adjust until every occupation's labor market clears. The economy has 551 occupations and about 136 million simulated workers, whose schooling and job histories decide which occupations they qualify for and can enter.

    An exposure index says which tasks go first. The observed-use index starts from where AI is used today and moves toward what the task labels rate as feasible; the other two apply assumed adoption fractions to published task ratings. The robot setting removes part of the time spent on tasks that a robot could do in an existing workplace. Intensity levels are listed by the share of all work time each one removes, because the indexes do not share a grid.

    What the model leaves out

    • AI and robots cost nothing and workers receive all of the gain, so the pay levels are upper bounds.
    • Nobody is unemployed: every worker holds the best-paid occupation open to them at the new wages.
    • New tasks for people appear only through the reinstatement dial of the observed-use index.
    • The workforce is today's. Retirements and new entrants, who shift the effect from pay toward employment over time, are not in these runs.
    • The largest levels stop short of removing every exposed task.

    Observed exposure and robot exposure: Anthropic Economic Index (CC-BY). Task labels: Eloundou et al. (2024). Task scores: Schaal (2025). Scenario dials: Korinek, Jones, Sacher, Cotter and McCrory (2026). Occupations and requirements: O*NET; employment and pay: BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics.
