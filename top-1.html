Exposure is not loss

In the simulation, AI raises nearly every worker’s pay. The workers it reaches most fall behind the rest.

The chart applies AI to today’s workers in steps, up to every task it is rated able to do: 46% of all work time, with output 149% higher. Almost no one is paid less in real terms at any step, and few change occupation. What moves is standing: at the top step 29% of workers rank five or more points lower in the pay distribution, and the most exposed quarter earn 112% more while falling 15% against the average. Pay here is an upper bound, because the model gives all the added output to workers; if 15% of it reached them, 9% would be paid less at full exposure.

The same prognosis, said two ways

People told the five-year survival rate expected to live 43% longer than people told the three-year rate for the same disease.

A doctor can describe one survival curve with different numbers: a three-year survival rate of 25%, a five-year rate of 10%, or a 37% chance of dying each year. In a randomized trial, people read a hypothetical diagnosis that gave one of the first two. The median expected survival was 3.0 years among those told the five-year rate and 2.1 years among those told the three-year rate. The result suggests that people weigh the time frame more heavily than the probability, so the date a doctor chooses to quote changes what a patient expects.

Trademark language

An Event-Dated Corpus of US Trademark Prosecution and a Two-Sided Measure of Vocabulary Position

2026

Working paper, under review.

  • SSRN
  • data on Zenodo
  • code on GitHub
  • online appendix
  • trademark explorer

Supersedes the earlier combined working paper, SSRN 6954598.

Patents count inventions, but most firms never patent; the goods-and-services language of trademark filings records what millions of firms tried to sell, dated to the day. This paper re-parses all 13.99 million US trademark case files into 242 million dated events, which turns the sworn five-year proof of continued use into a product-level survival outcome. On that corpus it builds two measures of each filing’s language: atypicality, how unusual the wording is for its industry, and lead, whether the wording ran ahead of or behind where the industry’s language was moving. The corpus, the measures and the code are public.

When Does It Pay to Be Early? Evidence from Every US Trademark Filing

2026

In preparation. The companion paper, on early entry.

  • IPO viewer

Strategy offers two opposite answers to whether it pays to bring a new kind of offering to market first: pioneers keep the markets they open, or most pioneers fail and followers take what they proved. Using the lead measure on every registration from 2002 to 2018, the paper finds that being early usually costs: leading descriptions are cancelled more often at the five-year proof, and leading firms are no more likely to raise money, list or be bought. The cost is concentrated where an industry’s filing volume is growing fast, in platform and network businesses, and among filers without counsel; in a few industries, such as beer, leading filings survive more often.

The future of work

An Occupation-Level Simulation of AI, Robots and the Labor Market

2026

Working paper, online.

  • summary
  • scenarios
  • workforce explorer

Most published estimates of AI’s effect on work give either a score for how exposed each occupation is, or a model of the whole economy with one or two kinds of worker. Neither says where displaced workers end up or what happens to their wages. This simulation is built from O*NET primitives (workers with graded capabilities, tasks with level requirements, jobs as bundles of tasks) and calibrated so the pre-shock equilibrium reproduces BLS employment and wages by construction. It solves for wages, prices and employment in 551 occupations with a simulated workforce that stands for 136 million workers, under the settings of the Anthropic Institute’s Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI. In the Institute’s substantial-change scenario, AI takes 7.7% of work time by 2030, output is 10% higher, real pay rises 9.7% in cognitive occupations and 12.1% in all others, and 0.3% of workers change occupation. Every assumption is a named, swappable module, so the question becomes which assumptions drive the answer rather than whose forecast to trust. Results can be read for the economy, for an occupation, or for workers who share schooling or work history.

Generating a Credible Synthetic Workforce: Skill Endowments from Observed Occupational Transitions

2026

Working paper, not yet circulated. Builds the simulated workforce that the simulation above runs on.

Any simulation of who can move to which job is only as good as its synthetic workers, and public data never observe an individual’s full skill vector. This generator builds one by requiring that every worker be qualified for the job they actually hold, then grows skills along career paths observed in longitudinal surveys, and checks the population against employment rates, age and education mixes, and churn. The hypothesis is that qualification-by-construction plus observed transitions recovers a workforce realistic enough to forecast on.

Decisions in healthcare and Medicaid

How Soon Is Not How Long: Separating Care Urgency from Housing Permanence in Disability Waiver Triage

2026

Working paper, under internal review.

Placing someone on a lifetime disability waiver is close to irreversible (a present-value commitment of several million dollars that almost never moves to cheaper care), yet the entry pipeline only asks how soon help is needed, never how long it will be needed. Twenty years of person-level records from one county show the two questions come apart: a measurable share of urgent need is temporary, and the reasons written on the intake form predict persistence better than the urgency score does. The proposal is to separate the triage of speed from the decision of permanence.

The Failure of Medicaid Effectiveness Measures

2026

In preparation. Why the measures a Medicaid system reports on itself do not track whether anyone got better.

Medicaid behavioral-health systems judge themselves on process measures (follow-up within seven days, a visit after discharge) that are cheap to compute and have little demonstrated relation to recovery. Using linked person-level claims and outcome records, the paper asks how much of the variation in whether anyone actually got better these measures explain, and what a measure built on observed outcomes would look like instead.

Communicating a Prognosis: a Randomized Trial of Survival Rate Language

2019

Silver, Broomell, Davis, White & Krishnamurti. Journal of General Internal Medicine.

  • free full text (PMC)

Patients routinely hear survival odds phrased in ways that change what they decide. A randomized trial tested whether framing a prognosis as survival or mortality, and as a percentage or a frequency, shifts how the same number is understood and acted on.

The Marriage Contract, 2027: The Prenup You Sign When You Don’t Sign a Prenup

2026; Kindle and paperback.

If you’re married, you already have a prenup; your state wrote it. Most of what the family code says is about how marriages end, because no court will referee one that’s still going, so the book also reads the law of being married: what marriage gives you, what it doesn’t, and the paperwork that fills the gap. Every clause appears twice, once as a lawyer would draft it and once in English.

Health: A User’s Guide: You Have a Part to Play in Your Own Care. Scripts Included.

2026; Kindle and paperback.

Part of health care is the people who look after you, and they mostly want what you want. The other part is an industry that pays people you'll never meet to decide what care you get and what you're charged. That side employs a revenue maximizer. Who's your cost minimizer? The book collects what a patient is already entitled to and gives the sentences to say, at a visit, in the hospital, and with a bill or a denial, with every claim sourced.
